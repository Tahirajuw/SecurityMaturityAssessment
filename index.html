Security Maturity Assessment

Answer 48 questions across six NIST CSF 2.0 functions. Scores are weighted by impact, critical controls can cap your level, and the roadmap is ordered by priority and dependencies. Control IDs are indicative NIST CSF 2.0 mappings. Answers save automatically in this browser, and critical controls cannot be marked not applicable.

How scoring works (methodology)

Answers. Each question is scored 0 (not in place) to 4 (optimised). Not applicable answers are excluded from scoring and the roadmap; critical controls cannot be excluded.

Weights. Every question has an impact weight of 1 to 3, set by the author's judgement: 3 for controls whose absence commonly enables or worsens serious incidents, 2 for important supporting controls. The weighted score is the sum of score times weight divided by the maximum possible, shown as a percentage.

Levels. Equal 20-point bands: below 20% Initial (1), 20-39% Developing (2), 40-59% Defined (3), 60-79% Managed (4), 80% and above Optimised (5). These bands are a simple convention, not derived from data, and are not the NIST Implementation Tiers.

Critical-control caps. The critical controls are . If any is scored Ad hoc or Not in place the level is capped at 2; if any is only Defined it is capped at 3. The reasoning is that strong results elsewhere should not hide a missing basic control.

Roadmap priority. Every control scored below Managed becomes an action. Priority is (3 minus score) times impact divided by effort, multiplied by 1.5 for critical controls and by 1.25 for domains boosted by the chosen sector. Sector boosts are judgement, not regulatory requirements.

Quarters. Actions are filled into Q1 to Q4 in priority order, with each quarter given an equal share of total effort. Prerequisites that are also gaps are scheduled ahead of the actions that need them, even when they rank lower on their own, and are listed first within a quarter.

Targets. Each domain has a target level (default Level 4, Managed), converted to the bottom of the score band for that level. Actions in a domain below target get a priority boost proportional to the gap; actions in domains already at or above target are scaled down to 0.6. The radar shows current against target.

Capacity. Each action has an effort rating of 1 to 3. The delivery capacity setting gives each quarter an effort budget (very small 3, small 6, medium 10, large 16 points; unconstrained spreads effort evenly). Actions are filled in priority order and anything beyond Q4 is listed as backlog. The budgets are assumptions, not measurements of real teams.

In progress. Controls marked as already planned or in progress still score as they are today, but their actions are listed separately, are not scheduled against capacity, and count as underway for dependency ordering.

Crosswalks. ISO 27001:2022 Annex A numbers and CIS Controls v8 numbers were checked against the published control lists. Which control applies to which question is the author's judgement, and CIS is mapped at Control level, not Safeguard level.

Mapping. Control IDs are indicative NIST CSF 2.0 subcategories, checked against a published list of the subcategories. NIST does not define these questions, scoring or levels.

Limits. This is a self-assessment screening tool. Weights, thresholds and caps have not been validated against real incident or audit outcomes, and results are only as accurate as the answers. It is not an audit or a full risk assessment.
